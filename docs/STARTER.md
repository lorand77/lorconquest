# Start here

LorConquest is a real-time game of kings and cities, from **1440 to 1650**. You rule one country; the computer rules the
rest. The full rules are in the other tabs — this page is what you need for your first game.

## Winning

Hold the number of settlements your scenario asks for (see **Scenarios**), counting your vassals' — a protectorate's count
half. If nobody gets there by the end year, the country with the most wins. If a computer country wins, every person loses.

## Time

A day passes every tenth of a second, a year in about 36 seconds. Alone you can pause at any time and play at ½×, 1×, 2× or
4×; with other people the clock stands still for a minute at the start to look around, and after that stops only when more
than half of the players vote for a pause — and any one of them may continue it. Orders take effect at once,
but armies take months to march.

## Money

Your settlements' people pay **tax** every month — set it from 2 to 3. Above 2 brings more money and a chance of peasants
rising. Soldiers cost upkeep. Land you conquer pays little at first: its people grow **compliant** over 20 years, and then a
**Courthouse** makes it your **core**, paying in full. Buildings — Workshops, Markets, Libraries, Walls — raise money,
research or defence. You may take loans; a treasury left deep in the red for ten years means bankruptcy.

## Research

Part of your money goes to research, by the **slider** (50% at the start). Pick a technology: when your research covers its
cost it unlocks — better guns (hand cannons, arquebuses, muskets), sharpshooters for mountain passes, and better ships.

## Armies

1. **Raise soldiers** in a core settlement — free, but they train for 3 months in your capital, 6 elsewhere, and how many
   you may have is capped by your **conscription law** (5% of your people at the start).
2. **Arm them**: soldiers without weapons are worth nothing in battle. Buy swords, pikes, cavalry, guns and cannons, and
   give them to an army anywhere.
3. **Move**: select an army and click where it should go. It follows roads, over passes and across straits (which need
   your ships in that sea).

Two enemy armies in one place fight a **battle**; the loser retreats. An empty enemy settlement falls the moment you enter
it. A defended one is **besieged** — it falls when the siege ends, unless relieved — or **stormed** at once, at a price.
Armies far from home in enemy land waste away if they are larger than the land can feed: split them up or bring them home.

## Diplomacy

You can only fight countries you are **at war** with, and only march through your own land, your allies' and your
enemies' — or a country's that gave you military access. Declaring war calls in your enemy's allies and guarantors.
Make **alliances**, **pacts** and **guarantees** in the Diplomacy tab; it shows what each computer country thinks of you
and whether it would agree. Peace can take land and money from the loser; a weaker country may agree to become your
**vassal** (it fights your wars) or **protectorate**. Attacking too often makes everyone wary of you — your **warmonger**
score.

## Ten things worth knowing

1. Raise armed men: choose the weapon when you raise them and the weapons are bought with them.
2. Pikes and guns together are stronger than either alone.
3. Walls double a siege's time; cannons shorten it.
4. Keep your capital: it trains men fastest and brings in a fixed sum every year.
5. Conquered land pays little for 20 years; plan for it.
6. A courthouse is only possible once the people comply; buy it then.
7. Libraries add up to half again to your research.
8. A higher law raises more men but cuts your money, and the highest ones make peasants rise.
9. Peace is per country: each ally of your enemy makes its own.
10. The Diplomacy tab shows a computer country's answer before you ask.
